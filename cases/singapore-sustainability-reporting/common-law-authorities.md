# Common law authority map for the Singapore sustainability-reporting case

**AI-prepared research aid, 26 October 2026. Unreviewed by an independent human lawyer.** This is an issue-selected first tranche of judgments, **not** an exhaustive list of common law cases, a third checked ledger proposition, or an assertion that any court has applied these decisions to a sustainability report. The [SGX listing-rule ledger](ledger.json) retains its existing two unreviewed propositions. ## Method and temporal scope

The three Singapore Court of Appeal judgments below were opened directly on Singapore Judiciary's eLitigation judgments site on 26 October 2026. They are historical decisions; the cited paragraphs state the reasoning in those decisions, **not a separately verified conclusion about every subsequent development as at 2026**. The UK decision is supplied as comparative common law background and is not treated as an independently binding Singapore rule. No source linked here decides the liability of an SGX issuer for sustainability reporting.

| Issue, party and necessary question | Opened primary judgment and pinpoint | Holding relevant to research; material limitation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Negligence; claimant against alleged maker of a statement:** Is a duty owed to *this claimant* for *this loss*? | [*Spandeck Engineering (S) Pte Ltd v Defence Science & Technology Agency* [2007] SGCA 37](https://www.elitigation.sg/gd/s/2007_SGCA_37), [77], [81]–[83] | Singapore's duty analysis starts with factual foreseeability, then proximity, then policy considerations capable of negating a prima facie duty. Reliance and assumption of responsibility may be relevant to proximity. **Qualification:** the appeal concerned a construction certifier, not an issuer's public ESG statement. A public statement alone does not establish a duty to every reader; breach, causation and loss would still require proof. |
| **Deceit; alleged recipient/investor:** Was an intentionally or recklessly false representation actually relied on? | [*Panatron Pte Ltd v Lee Cheow Lee* [2001] SGCA 49](https://www.elitigation.sg/gd/s/2001_SGCA_49), [19]–[24] | The Court accepted that a false representation can materially induce an investment even where other reasons also influenced it; the representation need not be the sole cause. Failure to take prudent verification steps does not by itself defeat established deceit. **Qualification:** a mistaken sustainability metric, without the requisite fraud and reliance, does not establish deceit; the claimants there were identified subscribers in a particular transaction. |
| **Directors' duties; company as claimant:** Did a director act honestly in the company's interests and with reasonable diligence? | [*Ho Kang Peng v Scintronix Corp Ltd* [2014] SGCA 22](https://www.elitigation.sg/gd/s/2014_SGCA_22), [35]–[42], [72] | Dishonest corporate conduct does not become proper merely because an asserted commercial benefit exists; care and diligence are assessed against the applicable directorial standard. **Qualification:** the case addressed unauthorised payments and a sham consultancy arrangement, not climate or sustainability disclosure. The company's claim against its director must not be confused with a shareholder's personal claim or an SGX disciplinary remedy. The judgment discusses Companies Act s 157 in its then-applicable 2006 revised edition; a present statutory allegation requires a separate check of current legislation and transitional application. |
| **English comparative foundation; negligent statement and reliance:** Was responsibility assumed towards the claimant, and was it excluded? | [*Hedley Byrne & Co Ltd v Heller & Partners Ltd* [1963] UKHL 4, [1964] AC 465](https://www.bailii.org/uk/cases/UKHL/1963/4.html), speeches of Lords Reid and Morris concerning assumption of responsibility, proximity and the 'without responsibility' disclaimer | The House of Lords considered liability for negligent misstatement and the significance of a special relationship and disclaimer. **Qualification:** a general published sustainability report is not necessarily advice assumed for a particular investor; the Singapore duty analysis must begin with *Spandeck*. This English judgment is persuasive comparative material, not a Singapore sustainability-reporting holding. |


## 28 October 2026: who may sue for a corporate disclosure wrong?

**Second, bounded source-inspection tranche.** This extension preserves the four original judgments above. It examines claimant identity, corporate versus personal injury, permission to bring a derivative action and the remedy sought. These are **Singapore judicial authorities interpreting company law and statutory proceedings**, rather than judgments establishing liability for an SGX sustainability report. The original two SGX ledger claims remain unreviewed. The analytical separation of reporting duty, representation, reliance and liability is consistent with [Tan, *Converging Standards, Divergent Accountability* (published 2026)](https://doi.org/10.1163/22134514-bja10122); that scholarship is an intellectual framework, not a holding of the courts.

| [*Ho Yew Kong v Sakae Holdings Ltd* [2018] SGCA 33](https://www.elitigation.sg/gd/s/2018_SGCA_33), [88]–[91], [115]–[117], [124]–[129] | The Court of Appeal distinguishes a member's **personal oppression** action under Companies Act s 216 from a **company-directed** action under s 216A. Where both are alleged, identify the real injury and essential remedy before selecting the route. | The successful s 216 claim concerned specific joint-venture legitimate expectations and commercial unfairness. Mere corporate loss passed through into share value is insufficient. At [115] the court cautions against using older overlap cases without regard to the distinct statutory derivative remedy. The later [*Zhuang Yujie*](https://www.elitigation.sg/gd/s/2026_SGHC_127) [1], [97] expressly refers to the personal/corporate distinction; this is **identified subsequent citation**, not exhaustive treatment. |
| [*Miao Weiguo v Tendcare Medical Group Holdings Pte Ltd* [2021] SGCA 116](https://www.elitigation.sg/gd/s/2021_SGCA_116), [193]–[197], [206]–[207] | Reflective loss is a rule concerned with shareholders **claiming as shareholders** for diminished share value or distributions flowing from an actionable wrong to their company. It does not bar every claim merely because the claimant happens to own shares. | The Court rejected double-recovery prevention as the entire rationale, and at [206] expressly reserved the possible *Giles v Rhind* exception. Its application requires the pleaded capacity and precise character of the loss. Later [*Khan Aisanullah v Rajib Kumar Dhali* [2024] SGHC 313](https://www.elitigation.sg/gd/s/2024_SGHC_313), [56], [68]–[70], applied the reflective-loss restriction and said that the exception's acceptance remained open; that is a **High Court application**, not a new Court of Appeal rule. |
| [*Petroships Investment Pte Ltd v Wealthplus Pte Ltd* [2016] SGCA 17](https://www.elitigation.sg/gd/s/2016_SGCA_17), [32]–[35], [67]–[73] | **Historical listing-status correction:** [67] records the July 2015 reform extending s 216A to Singapore-listed companies. A previous exclusion from the 1993 version cannot be carried into a 2026 Mainboard example. The appeal held that s 216A was **unavailable once the company was in liquidation**; [72] extends that position to either statutory or common law derivative action. | The court left open at [67]–[71] the availability of a parallel common law derivative route where the statutory route is available. Foreign incorporation, current statutory wording, insolvency status and who controls litigation require individual checks. Liquidator duties and court oversight are discussed at [73] and must not be confused with shareholder leave. |
| [*Zhuang Yujie v Openmeta Pte Ltd* [2026] SGHC 127](https://www.elitigation.sg/gd/s/2026_SGHC_127), [1]–[4], [52]–[54], [72]–[74], [97]–[98], [109] | A 15 June 2026 **General Division High Court** decision describes the s 216A permission stage: qualifying complainant, notice to directors, good faith, and prima facie corporate interest. It distinguishes personal loss of wallet access from potentially corporate loss of the assets. The permission assessment is not a trial of underlying liability. | The judge expressly recorded at [5] that the respondent had **appealed**. That appeal's eventual result was not established in this bounded source check. The holding concerns two particular companies and disputed crypto-assets, not a sustainability issuer. Neither a notice nor permission alone proves the intended claim succeeds. |
| [*Vivaz Group Holdings Pte Ltd v TripleOne (Cambodia) Investment Pte Ltd* [2026] SGHC(A) 20](https://www.elitigation.sg/gd/s/2026_SGHCA_20), [47], [50]–[51], [90]–[91] | The **Appellate Division** on 30 July 2026 dismissed the leave appeal for want of established good faith in the circumstances. It clarified that a failure of candour **alone** does not invariably defeat good faith, and that disputed facts at the permission stage are assessed without a full trial. | It concerns an alleged corporate asset transfer, with specific contradictory documentary evidence. Its good-faith and corporate-interest inquiries must not be conflated. This decision was independently inspected as a later appellate authority, not used to pronounce on the still-unverified appeal reported in *Zhuang*. |

### Application to the existing fictional teaching contrast

Keep Issuer A and Investor B wholly fictional. Before alleging that Investor B may personally recover a falling share price caused by a misstated emissions figure, require the reviewer to answer **(i)** whether the pleaded wrong is to A, B personally or both; **(ii)** whether B claims merely as a shareholder for reflective loss or has an independently actionable personal representation; **(iii)** whether the selected remedy vindicates that injury; **(iv)** whether corporate action requires leave, satisfies current company-type rules and is affected by liquidation or time limits; and **(v)** what source proves each element. *Ho Yew Kong*, *Miao Weiguo*, *Petroships*, *Zhuang* and *Vivaz* provide distinct judicial questions, **not an outcome for the fictional example**. No ESG claimant has been adjudicated in these judgments.

On the assumed pleaded facts at the striking-out stage, the court **struck out HLT's claimed trading losses as too remote** under contractual *Hadley v Baxendale* reasoning, without deciding that all other asserted auditor negligence claims succeeded or failed. | At [87]–[89] the court **reanalysed *Caparo*'s reasoning as concerning remoteness rather than an investor duty question**. ### Refined causal chain for the wholly fictional Issuer A example

This is a **proposed review method, not a court outcome**. Before claiming that a sustainability report's inaccurate emissions figure caused Investor B's trading loss or that assurer C must compensate it, identify separately: (1) the actual **defendant and claimant** and whether any contractual relationship exists; (2) which legal wrong is pleaded, including any direct representation to B; (3) the precise communication and B's **actual reliance or other statutory causal route**, not assumed reliance by all market participants; (4) the proposed breach and the acts expected of an otherwise competent professional; (5) a counterfactual with evidence about **what would have changed** and when; (6) other causes of price movement and loss; (7) the legally protected type of loss and **contractual or tortious remoteness**; (8) illegality, contributory fault, exclusion clauses and mitigation where properly available; (9) the remedial claimant, quantification and no double recovery. The investor, auditor and issuer are different actors. Do not treat an auditor-company duty as proof of an investor-facing duty.

For a **contractual assurance** inquiry, require the actual engagement letter, addressees, intended users, reliance restrictions, assurance standard and version, specified reporting boundary, period, performed procedures and contemporaneous communications. Also establish who accepted the contract and whether any recognised legal basis gives an outsider enforceable rights. **No such contract or relevant completed sustainability-assurance adjudication has been inspected.** The statutory-audit judgments provide *issue prompts*, not an adjudication of ESG assurance obligations.

## Decision tree for a *fictional* teaching scenario

**Fictional only:** Listed Issuer A publishes a sustainability report containing a materially inaccurate emissions figure. Investor B alleges a loss after reading it. No real issuer, report, assurance engagement, trading data, expert conclusion or court decision is supplied.

1. **Duty and supervision:** Identify financial year, issuer segment and correct version of Rules 711A, 711B, 703 and Practice Note 7.6. Ascertain any regulator's jurisdiction and actual enforcement power from applicable primary instruments before asserting supervision or a sanction. None of the four common law cases supplies these listing-rule powers.
2. **Fraud/deceit:** Establish the exact representation, falsity, knowledge or recklessness, B's actual inducement, transaction, causal loss and available relief. *Panatron* [19]–[24] concerns actual reliance, not presumed reliance by everyone who viewed a public report.
3. **Negligence:** Apply *Spandeck* [77], [81]–[83] to the claimant–defendant relationship; establish any actionable breach, causation and remoteness separately. The UK comparison does not displace Singapore law.
4. **Directors' duties:** Ask whether the alleged wrong is owed to the company and whether the company can prove breach and relief. *Ho Kang Peng* [35]–[42] is no automatic personal damages entitlement for B. Standing, derivative action requirements and the specific remedy require independent statutory and case-law investigation.
5. **Alternatives and qualifications:** Securities/statutory causes of action, contractual assurance obligations, market-loss calculations, limitation, defences, causation evidence and remedies are **not verified here**. A listing-rule breach is not automatically a common law tort; a false disclosure is not automatically fraud, personal standing or recoverable investment loss.

## Contrary considerations and review plan

* *Spandeck* [83] expressly requires consideration of policy that may negate a prima facie negligence duty. *Hedley Byrne* itself discusses the limiting effect of the disclaimer. These are substantive restrictions on expansive liability.
* *Ho Kang Peng* [37]–[38] protects bona fide business judgments from retrospective second-guessing, while distinguishing dishonest decisions. A failed forecast is not itself breach of directors' duties.
* The Singapore authorities must be checked for subsequent authoritative treatment before use in litigation or an article claiming the current exhaustive law. The SGX instruments and any present Companies Act/Securities and Futures Act provisions must separately be opened and temporally matched; nothing in this map verifies statutory standing or remedies.
