# Research standard

RegNav is a research project about the evidence behind legal propositions. Its
initial software records how a proposition relates to a source and where that
relationship can be examined. The value of a future legal dataset will depend
on the quality of the analysis entered into it.

## Source records

Prefer the issuing institution's legal text or judgment. Record the source's
identity, jurisdiction, version and reproducible pinpoint. Distinguish a rule's
publication date, commencement date and the date on which it was consulted.
Do not infer that a webpage's most recent edit changed the law.

When a translation is used, identify its status and check the original language
where the proposition depends on wording. An abstract, search snippet or
secondary summary is insufficient to establish a disputed passage.

## Interpretation and disagreement

State propositions narrowly. Separate the text of a legal rule from an
interpretation of its scope, and separate both from a proposal for reform.
Record qualifications and contrary authorities. A shared reporting standard
does not by itself establish identical supervision, liability or remedies.

Human review requires examination of the relevant passage in context. The
reviewer's declaration is part of the research record. A structural validator
cannot establish that the review occurred or that its conclusion is correct.
When a source, proposition or interpretation changes, reconsider the review.
Version 0.1 does not invalidate old review declarations automatically.

## Public examples

Training fixtures are labelled synthetic. Real worked examples must identify
their scope, source versions and unresolved questions. Never invent a court,
case number, quotation, DOI, legal proposition or reviewer. Links to an author's
published research provide intellectual context; they are not independent
validation of the software.

## Evaluation

Assess the software with observable tasks: finding a missing pinpoint,
identifying an unsupported proposition, preserving a disagreement and reproducing
an evidence matrix. Compare outcomes against an ordinary manual record before
claiming a benefit. Record the evaluator, materials, procedure and limitations.
Do not infer learning gains, legal accuracy or productivity from the number of
test cases or GitHub stars.

## Corrections and attribution

Keep corrections visible in version history. Give contributors appropriate
credit and cite the software version actually used. AI assistance in coding or
documentation does not constitute external peer review or the maintainer's
substantive endorsement of an automatically generated legal assertion.

For context, the [DORA recommendations](https://sfdora.org/read/) recognise
software and datasets as research outputs and call for assessment of their
substantive value. They do not certify this project's quality.
