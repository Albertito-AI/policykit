# Using an evidence ledger in research and editing

An evidence ledger is a record of reasoning that another person can inspect.
It is useful when a draft relies on multiple versions of a rule, draws a
comparative conclusion, or contains a proposition that combines several sources.

## Begin with a bounded question

Choose a short argument, a case note or one part of a paper. Identify the
propositions on which its conclusion depends. A proposition should state who,
what and when with enough precision to make disagreement possible. Split a
sentence if different parts require different authorities.

Record the source actually consulted. A judgment, an amended statute, an
unofficial translation and a commentary about the judgment are different
sources. Record their versions and jurisdictions separately. An access date
does not by itself establish that a source is current or authoritative.

## Trace the relation to the proposition

For each link, enter a reproducible location. A URL to a long document is rarely
a sufficient pinpoint. Distinguish material that supports the proposition from
material that narrows it, contradicts it or merely supplies context. Treat each
relation as an analytic judgment that a reviewer may revise.

Keep the proposition unreviewed while this work is incomplete. Human review
should examine the source text, its authority and temporal scope, the accuracy
of any quotation, and the inference drawn from it. A ledger entry cannot make
those judgments by being well formatted.

## Preserve disagreement

Contradictory evidence belongs in the record. A reviewer may narrow the
proposition, explain a difference between jurisdictions or versions, or leave
it contested. A written resolution should state why the contrary material
changes, or does not change, the argument. A successful validation run does not
resolve a conflict.

## Use the report in a review meeting

Inspect the evidence matrix alongside the sources and draft. Ask whether the
same proposition is supported throughout its stated scope, whether the
pinpoints can be reproduced, and whether another interpretation deserves to be
recorded. Record the reviewer and review date when an entry is marked checked.

After changing a proposition or replacing a source, repeat the relevant human
review. Version the JSON file with the research project if appropriate. This
prototype does not automatically reset old review statuses or retain history;
the researcher must manage those changes.

## Evaluate usefulness before making impact claims

A pilot can measure how long reviewers take to trace a defined sample of
propositions, how often they locate the intended passage, and what substantive
errors or interpretive disagreements they find. Compare a similar task without
the ledger, record sampling and exclusions, and report failures. Software test
results establish aspects of program behaviour; evidence of improved scholarly
review requires a separate evaluation.
